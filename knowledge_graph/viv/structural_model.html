VIV Branch 03 · structure → modes

Build the structural system that determines what the riser or umbilical can naturally do.

This branch follows your SHEAR7 study modules: geometry, tension, bending stiffness, mass, contents, buoyancy, attachments and boundary conditions are represented in the OrcaFlex structural model. The modal solution then provides natural frequencies, mode shapes and curvature-compatible modal data for SHEAR7.

Division of responsibility from your notes

  • OrcaFlex: defines the structural modes — what vibration patterns and natural frequencies are available.
  • SHEAR7 Stage II: later checks which of those modes can be energized by the current.
  • SHEAR7 Stage III: later solves the converged VIV motion using lift, damping, iteration and mode superposition.
Core rule: do not select an “excited mode” in this branch. This page creates the structural modal basis. Excitation comes only after the current-side shedding field is compared with the modal frequencies.

The study modules emphasize that displacement controls visible motion, while curvature controls bending strain and stress. Higher modes can therefore be fatigue-critical even when displacement is modest.

Transfer QA from Module 2

  • Coordinate positions and arc-length convention match SHEAR7.
  • Frequency units are consistent.
  • Current profile maps to the same structural positions.
  • Hydrodynamic diameter includes the intended coating / marine growth / suppression basis.
  • Added-mass assumptions are compatible.
  • Enough modes are exported to cover the possible shedding-frequency range.
  • High-mode shapes have adequate spatial resolution.
  • Mode-shape orientation and normalization are handled consistently.
Study-module distinction: OrcaFlex answers “what can the structure naturally do?” The next SHEAR7 matching stage asks “which of those modes can the current energize?”
